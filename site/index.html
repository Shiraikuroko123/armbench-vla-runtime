FIGURE 1 / EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN

Five futures from one handoff

A policy requests an action chunk. Four control periods later, the response arrives. The hand has kept moving for those 200 milliseconds. We restore the physical state, queued commands, camera inputs and policy sampling key at that instant.

Two branches act on the existing chunk. Three spend an additional call and differ in which observations they submit. A hybrid packet combines the earlier camera views with current hand readings. Comparing it with the fresh packet isolates the change in the two images.

What changes when the robot looks again?

Four pushing layouts and four bowl layouts supplied 37 eligible handoffs. Restoring each one and running the five branches produced 185 trajectories. The two comparisons below ask how newer images affect a query and whether making the query beats continuing.

FIGURE 3 / LOCAL REWRITING

One changed command can reverse a success

To separate row selection from rewriting the first translation, a development study ran four branches at each of 24 OpenVLA-OFT bowl handoffs. All branches used the same recorded continuation after the intervention window.

Direct age alignment succeeded 12 times. Hand-position row selection alone succeeded 11 times, age-aligned rewriting nine, and hand-position selection with rewriting eight. The combined rule lost four successes across three layouts and gained none.

25.33 mm In one repeated-command case, both selectors chose row 4. Rewriting only its translation moved the target by this distance and the task failed.

FIGURE 4 / TIMING THE REFRESH

Contact loss can have two endings

A separate study restored 45 eligible handoffs across approach, contact and transport. Its binary rule could continue the queue or request a fresh observation. It chose to refresh 20 times.

Two bowl layouts lost contact during an intervention. In one, a refresh recovered the task. In the other, both choices timed out. Across all 45 cases the rule recovered one success and lost one relative to continuing.

FIGURE 5 / ARTICULATED OBJECTS

The drawer moved. The success count held.

Two development layouts per task calibrated normal motion for the binary rule. Across 16 further layouts, continuing and refreshing produced the same outcome on all 64 handoffs. They succeeded on 31 of 32 drawer handoffs and all 32 stove-switch handoffs.

At the later window, holding the queued motion changed median drawer position by 20.16 mm and stove angle by 1.17° relative to normal waiting. The rule made 19 early queries for the drawer and eight for the switch, using 1,111 calls overall versus 1,101 for continuation.

FROM HANDOFFS TO COMPLETE EPISODES

Another query has a cost.

On 50 complete cream-cheese and wine-bottle episodes, the frozen rule triggered at every first eligible handoff. Over complete trajectories it made 869 additional calls and lost one success relative to the regular schedule.

Does one extra look help?

Refreshing at the first trigger produced the same 24 successes per task as regular execution. On 46 eligible controlled handoffs, current and older packets each succeeded 36 times. Two handoffs succeeded only with newer views and two only with older views.

Source: the frozen six-studies policy protocol for cream-cheese and wine-bottle tasks. The handoff study used the first six layouts per task and excluded two shifts that introduced new contact.
